# Decision quality versus outcome quality

## Synthesis

ProductOS should evaluate a decision primarily against the information, alternatives, values, uncertainty, and constraints available when commitment became costly to reverse. A favorable outcome does not by itself prove that the reasoning was sound, and an unfavorable outcome does not by itself prove that it was poor.

Original experiments by Baron and Hershey reported that people rated decision thinking and decision makers more favorably when identical uncertain decisions produced favorable outcomes. A later preregistered replication using a larger, between-participants sample reproduced the direction of this outcome bias in a medical scenario.

This does not mean outcomes should be ignored. Outcome information can reveal whether prior beliefs were calibrated and can update probabilities for future, sufficiently similar decisions. A useful review therefore separates two questions:

1. Was the original decision justified by what was knowable at commitment?
2. What does the observed outcome teach us for future decisions?

## ProductOS behavior

When reviewing a launch, experiment, investment, or roadmap decision, ProductOS should reconstruct the ex-ante evidence and uncertainty before considering the outcome. It should then use the outcome to test hypotheses, update beliefs, and improve future process without rewriting what the decision maker could have known.

## Limitations

The empirical evidence reviewed here comes primarily from hypothetical medical and monetary scenarios, not Product Management teams. The transfer is a reasoned application and must remain open to domain-specific evidence. The professional Decision Quality principles provide normative guidance rather than independent causal validation.
