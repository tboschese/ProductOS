# Review: RES-001 decision quality versus outcomes

**Status:** Editorial review required

## Scope

The slice remains bounded to evaluation of decisions under uncertainty. It does not claim to provide a complete decision-analysis framework or performance-management policy.

## Evidence assessment

- Baron and Hershey (1988) is original experimental work across five studies, but its participants and hypothetical medical/monetary scenarios limit direct generalization to product organizations.
- Aiyer et al. (2023) provides a preregistered, larger between-participants replication of one original medical scenario. It supports the direction of outcome bias while explicitly limiting generalization from a single scenario.
- The Society of Decision Professionals provides a current professional standard, not independent empirical proof. It is used for normative framing rather than causal claims.

## Contradiction and nuance

The original paper explicitly notes that outcome information can appropriately update beliefs about probabilities in repeated similar decisions. ProductOS should therefore avoid both outcome bias and the opposite error of ignoring outcome evidence during learning.

## Review gaps

- Independent editorial review has not occurred.
- Direct evidence in Product Management settings was not identified in this bounded slice.
- The six-element Decision Quality framework was not registered as a framework because its full origin, boundary conditions, alternatives, and evidence base were outside scope.

## Recommendation

Keep all extracted entities `in_review`. They are suitable for structural integration and eval design but should not be promoted to `approved` until an independent reviewer verifies locators, wording, and Product Management transfer limits.
