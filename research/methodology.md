# ProductOS research methodology

## Objective

Research exists to improve a concrete product decision, reasoning behavior, knowledge relationship, or eval. Volume is not an objective.

## Research contract

Every task defines:

- a stable ID and title;
- one research question;
- why the answer matters to ProductOS reasoning;
- explicit scope and out-of-scope boundaries;
- subtopics and questions;
- intended primary and secondary source classes;
- competing views to investigate;
- expected outputs;
- acceptance criteria and review state.

The `research-task` schema is the executable contract.

## Workflow

### 1. Intake

Search existing tasks, concepts, frameworks, terminology, and sources. Reject tasks that merely request broad coverage without a decision use case.

### 2. Source planning

Identify the strongest practical evidence before browsing broadly. Include original authors, primary research, standards, regulation, or product data where applicable. Record why weaker source types are necessary.

### 3. Collection

Register source metadata and precise locators. Separate notes from source claims and researcher interpretation. Do not infer missing metadata.

### 4. Analysis

Extract material claims, limitations, context, contradictions, and boundary conditions. Compare competing schools of thought without manufacturing consensus.

### 5. Synthesis

Explain what the evidence supports, what remains unknown, when guidance applies, and what decisions it can improve. Propose concepts, frameworks, decision patterns, anti-patterns, terminology changes, and eval cases only when supported.

### 6. Review

A reviewer checks source quality, claim accuracy, contradictions, duplication, terminology, copyright, and the task's acceptance criteria. Authors should not approve their own high-impact claims when independent review is available.

### 7. Publication

Approved outputs update canonical entities. Research notes remain provenance, not an alternative knowledge base. Any reasoning change includes an eval proposal and regression run.

## Definition of done

A task is complete only when:

- its question is answered within scope;
- primary sources were investigated where practical;
- strong secondary evidence and competing views were considered;
- source locators and limitations are recorded;
- proposed canonical entities were deduplicated and reviewed;
- terminology and relationships were considered;
- relevant evals were added or explicitly deemed unnecessary;
- no restricted or confidential source material was committed.

## Review outcomes

Review state is one of `draft`, `in_review`, `approved`, or `rejected`. Rejected work remains traceable with a reason and may return to draft after material revision.
