# Frameworks

Frameworks are tools, not answers. Use one only when it improves the decision, explain why it
fits, and say when it would mislead. When the user asks for a framework, check whether it suits
the problem and propose a better fit if not.

## Prioritization

- **RICE / ICE scoring:** useful to compare many similar-sized items and expose assumptions.
  Misleads when estimates are guesses presented as data, or when items differ in kind, such as
  strategic bets versus small fixes. Show the uncertainty in the inputs.
- **Cost of Delay / WSJF:** useful when timing changes value, such as deadlines, competition, or
  compounding costs. Requires an honest estimate of value over time.
- **MoSCoW:** useful to negotiate scope within a fixed release. Weak for choosing between
  opportunities.
- **Kano:** useful to separate basic expectations from performance and delight attributes, with
  real customer data. Weak as a desk exercise.
- **Opportunity cost and strategic fit** usually matter more than any score; a scoring model
  does not replace a strategic choice.

## Discovery and customer understanding

- **Jobs to be Done:** useful to describe the progress a customer seeks independently of the
  solution. Misused when "jobs" are invented without evidence.
- **Opportunity Solution Tree:** useful to connect an outcome to opportunities, solutions, and
  assumption tests, and to avoid jumping to one solution.
- **Assumption mapping:** useful to find the riskiest assumption before building; pairs with the
  discovery reference.
- **Personas:** useful only when grounded in research and tied to decisions; fictional personas
  add false confidence.

## Strategy and business model

- **Strategy as choices** (where to play, how to win, what not to do) and **diagnosis, guiding
  policy, coherent actions:** useful to test whether a strategy makes real choices.
- **Business Model Canvas / Lean Canvas:** useful to make a business model's assumptions visible
  on one page; not evidence that the model works.
- **Porter's Five Forces, SWOT:** useful for structured market context; SWOT in particular tends
  toward unprioritized lists.

## Metrics and growth

- **North Star metric with input metrics:** useful to align teams on a value-indicating behavior;
  needs guardrails against gaming.
- **AARRR (pirate metrics):** useful as a funnel checklist for growth; can hide that retention
  drives the rest.
- **HEART:** useful for choosing user-experience metrics by goal, signal, and metric.

## How to apply

State the decision the framework serves, apply it to the user's actual context, show where
inputs are assumptions, and give the conclusion in plain language. Do not present a filled
template as analysis.
