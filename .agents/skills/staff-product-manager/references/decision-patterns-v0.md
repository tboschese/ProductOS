# Initial decision patterns

These patterns provide decision prompts, not fixed recipes.

## Retention decline

Before selecting a solution:

- validate retention definition, instrumentation, and comparison window;
- compare cohorts, acquisition sources, segments, activation, time-to-value, engagement, and churn timing;
- review qualitative churn, support, and Customer Success evidence;
- distinguish mix shift, measurement change, product value, quality, price, and competitive explanations;
- connect proposed action to the evidence explaining churn.

Do not treat aggregate retention change as proof of a feature gap or causal product regression.

## Enterprise feature request

Distinguish:

- the user problem from the requested implementation;
- user, buyer, procurement, security, and contractual needs;
- one deal's value from reusable market value;
- reversible accommodation from permanent product complexity;
- revenue upside from delivery, support, precedent, and opportunity costs.

Compare product solution, service/process workaround, integration, configuration, pilot, commercial exception, and declining the request.

## Strategy and OKRs

Before drafting goals, establish strategic choices, current outcomes, constraints, time horizon, and the behavior or business result that should change.

Objectives describe meaningful direction. Key Results measure outcomes or credible leading indicators. Projects and milestones may support KRs but should not masquerade as outcomes.

Check for metric gaming, conflicting KRs, missing baselines, absent owners, and links to incentives or performance evaluation.

## Legacy modernization

Clarify the business and user reason for modernization. Map domain boundaries, business rules, dependencies, data, reliability, security, regulation, operating cost, change readiness, and migration risk.

Compare incremental modernization, strangler replacement, platform extraction, vendor replacement, containment, and deliberate maintenance. Do not default to a rewrite or a fashionable architecture.

Define continuity, rollback, data integrity, adoption, and business-outcome measures.

## AI feature proposal

Begin with the user decision or workflow and the value of probabilistic automation. Establish a non-AI baseline and compare simpler alternatives.

Evaluate task success, error severity, evaluation data, hallucination, privacy, safety, latency, cost, observability, human oversight, feedback loops, and model change risk.

Separate demo quality from production reliability. Define golden datasets, acceptance thresholds, guardrails, escalation, and ongoing evaluation before scaling.
