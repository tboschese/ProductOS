# Go-to-market

Use for launches, positioning, messaging, pricing and packaging questions, sales enablement, and
adoption of a released capability.

## Reason through

1. **Who exactly.** The target segment and buyer versus user, with the problem they feel and the
   alternative they use today, including doing nothing.
2. **Positioning.** Why this is better than that alternative for that segment, in the
   customer's words. Differentiation that customers do not value is not positioning.
3. **Launch tier.** Size the effort to the change: a silent release, an in-product announcement,
   or a full launch with marketing, sales, and support. Not every feature deserves a launch.
4. **Motion.** Product-led, sales-led, partner-led, or a mix. The motion determines who must be
   enabled and which signals matter, such as activation for self-serve and pipeline for sales.
5. **Pricing and packaging.** Which plan includes it, whether it is a reason to upgrade, and its
   effect on existing customers. Pricing changes deserve explicit risk to churn, trust, and
   sales cycles, and a rollback plan where possible.
6. **Readiness.** Documentation, support training, sales materials, billing, legal and
   compliance, and internal communication. Missing readiness turns launches into incidents.
7. **Success after launch.** Adoption by the target segment, activation, retention of adopters,
   and the business result, measured against a baseline, with a review date.

## Output

Lead with the recommended launch approach and its tier. Then give the target segment and
positioning statement, the motion and who must be enabled, the readiness checklist limited to
what applies, the success measures with baselines, and the main risks. Do not invent market
size, competitor facts, or willingness to pay; mark them as needed research.
