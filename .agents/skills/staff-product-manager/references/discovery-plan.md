# Planning a discovery

Use when the user is unsure what to investigate, how, or whether discovery is needed at all.
Discovery exists to change a decision; a plan that would not change any decision is waste.

## Reason through

1. **Decision at stake.** What will be decided after discovery, by whom, and by when? If no
   decision depends on it, say so.
2. **What is already known.** Separate the user's facts, observations, assumptions, and
   opinions. Do not treat a hunch or a stakeholder's belief as a finding.
3. **Dominant uncertainty.** Name the one or two unknowns most likely to change the decision,
   classified as value, usability, feasibility, viability, or market risk.
4. **Competing hypotheses.** List credible explanations or bets, including the uncomfortable
   one, and what each would predict.
5. **Smallest sufficient method.** Choose the cheapest method that discriminates between the
   hypotheses: existing data, support tickets, sales notes, interviews, observation,
   prototype test, fake door, concierge, or experiment. Explain why it fits and what it cannot
   show. Do not default to interviews.
6. **Who and how many.** Which segment, why, and how recruited; avoid sampling only friendly or
   loud customers.
7. **Decision rule.** Before collecting, state what result would lead to each option, and when
   to stop.
8. **Proportion to stakes.** Reversible, low-cost choices may need a quick check or none;
   hard-to-reverse or expensive ones justify more evidence.

## Output

Lead with the recommended next step in one or two sentences. Then give:

- the decision and the dominant uncertainty;
- hypotheses and what would distinguish them;
- the plan: method, participants or data, duration, and owner, sized to the stakes;
- for interviews, five to eight questions about past behavior, not opinions about the idea
  ("Tell me about the last time…", not "Would you use…");
- the decision rule and stop criteria;
- what the plan cannot answer.

Avoid generic discovery checklists, persona templates, and framework names unless they change
what the user should do.
