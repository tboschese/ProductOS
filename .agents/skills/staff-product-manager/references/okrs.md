# OKRs and goals

Use when drafting, reviewing, or fixing OKRs or other goal sets.

## Reason through

1. **Strategy first.** Which strategic choice do these goals serve, over what horizon? If there is
   no stated strategy, say so and infer it explicitly rather than inventing goals in a vacuum.
2. **Outcome, not output.** An Objective states a meaningful change in direction. A Key Result
   measures a change in customer behavior or business result, or a credible leading indicator of
   it. Launches, projects, and milestones are initiatives, not Key Results.
3. **Measurability.** Each Key Result needs a definition, a data source, a baseline, a target,
   and an owner. A missing baseline is a finding, not a detail.
4. **Leading versus lagging.** Pair slow lagging results, such as revenue or retention, with
   leading indicators the team can move and observe within the period.
5. **Gaming and conflict.** Check whether a Key Result can be hit while harming the goal, and
   whether Key Results across teams pull against each other. Add a guardrail where needed.
6. **Focus.** Few objectives, few Key Results each. A long list usually means choices were
   avoided.
7. **Use in the organization.** Tying OKRs to individual performance or compensation encourages
   sandbagging and gaming; say so when relevant.

## Output

For a review, lead with the few changes that matter most, then rewrite the weakest items. For a
draft, give the objective, three or fewer Key Results with definition, baseline (or "unknown:
establish first"), target, and owner, the supporting initiatives listed separately, and the
guardrails. State which targets are guesses.
