# Entity extraction

Create an entity only when it has a stable meaning, a clear owner, and a use in reasoning or evaluation.

## Sources

Register identity, access, authority class, facets, summary, limitations, and verification date. A source record is metadata, not endorsement.

## Claims

Write one material proposition per claim. Assign epistemic status and evidence modality. Link supporting and contradicting source references with precise locators. Keep confidence qualitative and explain limitations.

## Concepts

Create a concept when multiple claims need a stable shared definition. Link claim IDs; do not copy their evidence into the concept.

## Frameworks

Require origin, problem, inputs, process, outputs, assumptions, use and avoid conditions, limitations, misuse, alternatives, complements, evidence, and sources. A named list or practitioner habit is not automatically a framework.

## Terminology

Map preferred labels and aliases to one canonical concept ID. Preserve established English names when that matches practitioner usage. Flag ambiguous aliases.

## Relationships

Create one canonical subject–predicate–object entity. Do not duplicate an editable relationship inside the linked entities.

## Decision patterns and anti-patterns

Decision patterns describe recurring decision structures and discriminating evidence. Anti-patterns describe a harmful reasoning behavior, trigger conditions, consequences, and corrections. Link both to eval cases when observable behavior can be tested.

## Evals

Propose cases that exercise judgment under ambiguity. Declare expected and forbidden behaviors, hard failures, and applicable rubric dimensions. Avoid trivia or answers that depend on exact wording.
