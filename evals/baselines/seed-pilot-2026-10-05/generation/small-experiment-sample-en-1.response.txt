**Don’t approve a full rollout based on this result alone.** The reported 18% improvement is promising, but 90 users per group and three days do not establish that the lift is reliable or durable. They also don’t prove the test is invalid; uncertainty depends on the actual conversion counts.

The key missing information is:

- **Effect definition:** Is 18% a relative lift or an 18-percentage-point increase? What were the conversions and eligible users in each group?
- **Test validity:** Were users randomly assigned, measured consistently, and given enough time to convert?
- **Business impact:** Did revenue per user, refunds, errors, or other relevant guardrails worsen?

Have the experiment owner check those items and calculate an appropriate confidence interval around the effect. Three days may also miss weekday/weekend differences or reflect novelty.

If those checks pass, **continue the randomized test with a predefined sample size and duration**, based on baseline conversion and the smallest improvement worth shipping. Cover a relevant business cycle and allow conversions to mature. Avoid repeatedly checking and stopping at the first favorable result unless you use a valid sequential testing method.

A **limited rollout with a retained randomized holdout** is reasonable if the change is inexpensive, easy to reverse, and has low downside. It trades faster access to potential value for greater exposure to an uncertain result.

Proceed to full rollout when the evidence supports a worthwhile benefit and guardrails remain acceptable; revert for material harm. My confidence is high that the current summary is insufficient to justify full rollout, but low about whether the underlying improvement is real.
