**Product should lead a decision about which business and clinical outcomes require modernization, then select the safest credible path to achieve them.** Do not approve a full microservices rewrite on the evidence provided. Favor incremental modernization as the starting option, subject to a focused assessment.

The only supplied evidence is the system’s age and Engineering’s proposal. Twenty years of use may mean accumulated complexity, but it may also mean embedded clinical rules and dependable workflows. Age alone establishes neither the need for replacement nor the suitability of microservices. Confidence is high in this decision approach; confidence in any architecture choice is currently low.

Product should lead five actions:

1. **Define the problem and decision authority.** Identify the concrete failures modernization must address: delayed clinical changes, unreliable workflows, security exposure, unsupported infrastructure, excessive operating cost, or poor usability. These are candidate problems, not established facts. Name an accountable executive sponsor; Product owns outcome priorities and trade-offs, Engineering owns technical assessment, and clinical, operational, security, and compliance leaders define required safeguards.

2. **Test competing explanations before selecting architecture.** Slow delivery might result from tightly coupled code, but also from unclear ownership, inadequate tests, release processes, or poorly understood business rules. Reliability problems might arise from infrastructure or data quality. Microservices could help where independent deployment and scaling matter; they also introduce distributed failure modes and operational demands. Require Engineering to connect its proposal to observed constraints and demonstrate readiness to operate it.

3. **Compare credible alternatives against the same outcomes.**

   | Option | Potential benefit | Principal trade-off |
   |---|---|---|
   | Maintain and harden | Addresses urgent reliability or security needs with limited disruption | May leave structural constraints unresolved |
   | Modularize and replace incrementally | Improves selected domains while preserving continuity | Requires careful boundaries and temporary coexistence |
   | Extract selected services | Enables independence where it has demonstrated value | Adds integration and operational complexity |
   | Replace with a vendor | May reduce ownership of commodity capabilities | Introduces fit, migration, and supplier dependencies |
   | Full rewrite | Can remove pervasive constraints | Concentrates migration risk and delays value until substantial replacement works |

4. **Run a time-bounded assessment and bounded pilot.** Map critical clinical journeys, business rules, integrations, data ownership, dependencies, and failure consequences. Validate baseline delivery, reliability, workflow, and cost measures. Select a useful domain with manageable clinical risk and test the proposed approach through an actual change and release. Include coexistence costs and the clinical improvements displaced by modernization in the investment case. Agree the assessment deadline and decision criteria before starting.

5. **Make migration evidence a release condition.** Clinical and technical owners should approve continuity plans, data reconciliation, rollback rehearsals, and workflow validation. Security and compliance specialists should assess applicable requirements. Establish clear data authority during coexistence; avoid conflicting writes or ambiguous clinical records.

Measure success through the outcomes that justified the investment: faster safe changes, fewer workflow failures, better clinician task completion, or lower total operating cost. Set thresholds from validated baselines, with guardrails for patient safety, data integrity, availability, and support burden. Service count and code rewritten are delivery measures, not success measures.

Reconsider a full rewrite if evidence shows incremental routes cannot meet essential requirements and a credible migration plan can preserve clinical continuity. Reconsider microservices if the pilot demonstrates meaningful independent deployment benefits that outweigh their operating costs. **The next commitment should fund this assessment and pilot, with an explicit review gate before broader migration.**
