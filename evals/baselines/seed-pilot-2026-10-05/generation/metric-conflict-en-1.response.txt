**Do not accept the claim that the product is healthier yet.** Weekly active use rising 25% and declining 90-day retention can coexist: more people may arrive or return this week while fewer stay over time. Neither metric alone establishes product health.

The supplied figures are observations; “engagement proves health” is an interpretation. Confidence is high that this conclusion is unsupported, but low about what caused the retention decline.

Three credible explanations need separating:

| Hypothesis | Evidence that would distinguish it |
|---|---|
| Acquisition growth brings in users with lower retention, changing the overall mix. | Retention is stable within comparable acquisition channels and customer segments, but their proportions shift. |
| Existing segments receive less durable value despite higher short-term activity. | Retention falls within comparable cohorts, supported by weaker activation, slower time-to-value, quality problems, or churn feedback. |
| Measurement or comparison differences create the apparent conflict. | Changes in event definitions, identity tracking, reporting windows, or incomplete 90-day cohorts explain the movement. |

**Next action:** have Product Analytics and Growth reconcile the metrics this week, with the product lead owning the resulting decision.

- Verify what counts as “active” and “retained,” the comparison periods, instrumentation changes, cohort sizes, and whether every retention cohort has completed 90 days.
- Break weekly active growth into new, continuing, and resurrected users. Compare retention within matched acquisition channels and segments; inspect both retention rates and absolute retained-user counts.
- If comparable cohorts show deterioration, examine activation, meaningful value-producing actions, churn timing, and support or cancellation evidence before choosing a solution.

Continue reversible work, but do not expand growth investment **on the strength of this health claim**. Expansion becomes more defensible if comparable cohorts maintain retention and acquisition economics remain acceptable. If deterioration persists within cohorts, prioritize its demonstrated cause.
