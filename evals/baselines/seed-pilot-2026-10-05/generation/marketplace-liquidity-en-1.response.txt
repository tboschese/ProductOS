**Hold the proposed increase in acquisition spend until you identify where matching deteriorated.** Both sides growing 40% does not establish that compatible supply and demand grew together. More acquisition could help an underserved segment—or amplify an existing bottleneck.

The supplied figures show growth and declining successful matches per user; they do not establish a cause. Total successful matches might still have increased. Confidence is moderate in delaying broad expansion, low in any specific diagnosis.

Three explanations deserve testing:

- **Measurement or cohort mix:** the denominator includes more new or inactive users, or newcomers have had less time to match.
- **Local imbalance:** growth differs by geography, category, availability, or other compatibility requirements, despite equal aggregate growth.
- **Matching or fulfillment friction:** relevant supply and demand exist, but discovery, response, acceptance, cancellations, or capacity prevent successful matches.

Have Product and Analytics complete a focused review within one week, with Marketing and marketplace Operations:

1. Validate “successful match,” the user denominator, instrumentation, and comparable observation windows. Separate supply-side and demand-side users.
2. Compare established users and equally mature acquisition cohorts, then segment by the marketplace’s main compatibility constraints and acquisition channel.
3. Locate the funnel loss—from eligible request through available supply, response, acceptance, and completion—and inspect failed-match reasons.

Use the findings to choose the investment:

| Finding | Next action |
|---|---|
| Available supply lacks relevant demand | Test targeted demand acquisition |
| Demand lacks compatible supply | Acquire or activate supply in those segments |
| Compatible participants fail to complete matches | Fix the identified friction before scaling |
| Cohort mix explains the decline and economics remain healthy | Consider expanding acquisition |

Any spend test should have a capped budget, a comparable control, and a predefined review window. Judge it on **incremental completed matches and cost per incremental completion**, with fulfillment rate, time to match, cancellations, and participant retention as guardrails.

Approve broader spend only when evidence supports the acquired side and segment, and the test improves matching with acceptable economics. More users alone is insufficient justification.
