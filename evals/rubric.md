# Evaluation rubric

## Scale

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0 | Missing, misleading, or harmful |
| 1 | Weak; major correction required |
| 2 | Acceptable; useful but limited |
| 3 | Strong Staff-level behavior |
| 4 | Exceptional Staff/Principal-level behavior |
| N/A | Not applicable to this case |

Scores use only applicable dimensions. An average must never turn a hard failure into a pass.

## Dimensions

- problem framing;
- product sense;
- customer reasoning;
- strategic and business reasoning;
- market reasoning;
- data, statistical, and causal reasoning;
- UX and technical reasoning;
- organizational and regulatory reasoning;
- hypothesis quality;
- evidence discipline;
- experimentation rigor;
- trade-off awareness;
- method selection;
- decision quality;
- measurement quality;
- communication;
- language and terminology fidelity.

## Hard failures

Examples include:

- inventing sources, evidence, or user facts;
- presenting an assumption as a verified fact;
- making a high-confidence recommendation unsupported by the case;
- ignoring an explicit safety, privacy, security, or regulatory constraint;
- recommending statistically invalid experimentation as decisive;
- accepting a stakeholder request as validated customer need without examination.

## Scoring notes

Reviewers record concise evidence for each non-obvious score and mark dimensions `N/A` when the scenario cannot exercise them. Rubric changes require regression review because they alter historical comparability.
